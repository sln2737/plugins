PROMPT

Write a scholarly op-ed on the topic:
“[TOPIC]”

The piece must meet the following standards:

1. Core Objective

Produce a publication-ready op-ed that is:

intellectually rigorous
argument-driven
grounded in reality
written in a clear, human, authoritative voice

The output must not read like:

a blog
a textbook
a whitepaper
a bullet-point summary

It must read like:

a sharp, informed thinker addressing an intelligent public audience.

2. Structural Discipline (Non-Negotiable)
No Roman numerals
No bullet lists
No modular headings like a report
Maintain continuous narrative flow

Structure implicitly as:

Opening hook (strong, provocative, non-generic)
Clear thesis (early, explicit)
Argument development (progressive, not fragmented)
Engagement with opposition (must challenge a real or implicit counter-view)
Real-world anchoring (events, systems, or observable realities — no fabrication)
Escalation of stakes (why this matters now)
Sharp, provocative closing (not summary — a push)
3. Argumentation Requirements
Prioritize argument over explanation
Every paragraph must advance the thesis
Avoid descriptive padding
Avoid repeating obvious facts

Must include:

at least one first-principles framing
at least one clear causal chain
at least one non-obvious insight
4. Tone and Voice
Assertive, but not theatrical
Precise, not verbose
Engaged, not detached
No motivational language
No rhetorical fluff
No exaggerated claims

The writer must sound like:

someone who understands the system deeply and is willing to take a position

5. Reality Constraints (Critical)
Do not fabricate citations, studies, or statistics
Do not invent examples
If uncertain, generalize carefully without false precision
Prefer:
structural truths
widely observable phenomena
known institutional dynamics
6. Editorial Quality Filters

Reject outputs that:

over-structure (like “Section I, II…”)
rely on lists instead of prose
explain instead of argue
lack tension or conflict
sound generic or templated
7. Language Precision
Avoid filler phrases
Avoid clichés
Avoid repetition
Avoid vague qualifiers (“very”, “somewhat”, etc.)

Every sentence must:

add meaning
move the argument
or sharpen the claim
8. Ending Requirement

The conclusion must:

not summarize
not soften
not drift

It must:

land a decisive intellectual strike or reframing that forces reconsideration of the topic.

9. Output Standard

The final piece should:

hold attention of a full auditorium
withstand scrutiny from domain experts
be suitable for publication in a serious outlet
include a data dense rich narrative supported with citations if asked.

END PROMPT
